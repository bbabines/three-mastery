# Photoreal effects

One page per real-time effect on the classic watch: what it adds, what it costs on your own machine, and when to turn it on. The effects are the ones WebGi uses for its look, rebuilt with r186's own pieces. The plan is in `docs/concept-inventory.md` (Domain 20), and the page format in `docs/writing-pages.md`.

Open `/harness/?drill=electives/effects/bounced-light`. The elective appears in the Look dev and Everything tracks.

## The bench

`stage.ts` loads the watch from `assets/reference/classic-watch/` (see `electives/product/README.md` for where it comes from) onto a light-gray floor, lit by its studio EXR at WebGi's saved intensity and rotation plus one shadowed key light. A matte card can stand beside it. Each page builds its own `RenderPipeline` and draws through `stage.draw`.

The stage puts back two of WebGi's settings that standard glTF loses, as the Product stage does: the strap's leather normal map and each material's saved `depthWrite` and normal scale. It changes two things for the pipeline:

- The printed numerals plane is a blended square. A scene pass that writes several buffers blends only the color, so its clear corners would stamp a gray square into the normals and everything read from them. The stage turns it into a cutout (`alphaTest = 0.5`), which also has to write depth.
- See-through parts don't cast shadows, since a shadow map has no blending.

## Timing

Since Oct 14, 2026 every cost scene has a **time it** button (`costMeter` in `stage.ts`; the budget page calls `stage.timeIt` for each of its states). `timeIt` draws the scene's own frame outside the animation loop, starting a new node frame for each (`renderer._nodes.nodeFrame.update()`, as the loop does, or passes skip themselves). First a warm-up of at least 500 ms of back-to-back frames, which also covers shader compiles and gets an idling GPU up to speed. Then five rounds of about 150 ms each, frames back to back with one wait for the GPU at the end of each round (`queue.onSubmittedWorkDone()` on WebGPU, a fence polled between short yields on WebGL 2); the middle round is the **cost**. Then 20 frames one at a time, each waited for, which adds the round trip and usually reads longer. While one scene times, every scene on the page holds still. Each readout also shows the last frame's passes, draws, triangles, and pixels, which don't wander.

The stage used to show r186's GPU timestamps (`trackTimestamp`, `resolveTimestampsAsync`), now removed along with the `timing: gpu` frontmatter. That total adds up each pass's duration, and on the M4 Pro the passes overlap: a later pass starts almost at once and ends when the one before it ends, so the sum counted the same time several times. Reflections at high quality, pixel ratio 2, read 121–152 ms against 16.5 ms per frame; bounced light at medium, ratio 1, 13–15 against 3.2; the WebGi preset 97–124 against 11.2. It also read low where the CPU was the limit: SSAA ×16 at ratio 1 read 1.1 ms for frames that took 7.4 (404 draws). On WebGL 2 the timer queries can't nest, so passes inside passes raised "beginQuery: a query is already active" and went untimed. (Found by an independent review on Oct 13, 2026, which timed frames by hand next to the readout; its scripts are in the git-ignored `.pick/review/effects-audit/` on Brad's Mac.)

Times still wander by up to a third from run to run on the M4 Pro, so the pages say to time each setting twice and trust only bigger gaps, and that only a phone measurement says what a phone can do.

## Progressive smoothing, ambient occlusion, reflections, contact shadows, smoothing while moving

Built Oct 12, 2026, in `progressive/`, `ambient-occlusion/`, `reflections/`, `contact-shadows/`, and `motion-smoothing/`.

- **Two scenes, one glass.** In r186, transmission copies the picture into a texture made per render target by cloning one shared `FramebufferTexture`, and clones share their size. Two scenes at different pixel ratios (every page's cost scene starts at 1), or SSAA's own sample target, resize it back and forth each frame, and WebGPU reports "Destroyed texture used in a submit" or an out-of-range copy. `separateFramebufferCopies()` in `stage.ts` gives each clone its own size. Since Oct 13, 2026 `effectsStage` calls it itself, so every page has it (see "Bench fixes" below).
- **Averaging still frames** has no r186 node: `progressive/smoothing.ts` has `AccumulateNode` (a running average with camera shifts from a 32-spot Halton list; its 'blend' mode is the no-motion-buffer comparison on the smoothing-while-moving page) and `magnify()`, the square-pixel close-up both pages use.
- **TRAA and MSAA** can't be combined: TRAA copies the scene pass's depth, and with `samples: 4` WebGPU reports a sample-count mismatch every frame (tried on the smoothing-while-moving page, then reverted).
- **Ambient occlusion** runs at full size: at half size (three.js's suggestion) the grain on the watch shows through TRAA. Its TRAA nodes set `useSubpixelCorrection = false`, as three.js's GTAO example does.
- **Reflections** are added on top of the picture, as three.js's SSR example does, so they can only brighten; the scene starts with the studio dimmed so the floor reflection reads. In mirror mode r186 doesn't fade hits at the screen's edge (only the stochastic mode does).
- **Contact shadows** barely show under the studio's light, so the scene starts on key light only. When this page was built, the key light's shadow camera kept three.js's default `near` 0.5 and `far` 500, so the bench's `bias` of −0.0005 was about 0.25 world units, a large share of why parts floated. The stage now fits it (below), and the page compares the two.
- **Checked** in headless Chrome through Playwright (`channel: 'chrome'`, progress and track writes intercepted), on WebGPU and `&backend=webgl`: every button clicked twice through with no console errors, each scene looked at by eye, and costs measured with only one scene on screen.

## Baked soft ground shadow, projected HDRI ground, brushed metal, glass

Built Oct 12, 2026, in `ground-shadow/`, `hdri-ground/`, `anisotropy/`, and `transmission/`. These pages draw the scene straight to the canvas (`transmission/draw.ts`, which reports each frame's draw and triangle totals), since their effects aren't post-processing. Their scenes set the crystal to one side (`transmission/crystal.ts`) unless the page is about it.

- **The bake** (`ground-shadow/bake.ts`) is the jewelry elective's `bakedGroundShadow` for WebGPURenderer: a cast-only copy of the watch, a `ShadowNodeMaterial` floor, a light moved over a cone, and a running average through a `QuadMesh`. The baked picture is read with `uv().flipY()`; without it the shadow came out mirrored front to back (seen on WebGPU). The overlay sits 0.004 above the floor rather than using `polygonOffset`.
- **GPU timing doesn't separate these effects on a desktop.** On the M4 Pro every setting of all four pages read 0.1 to 3 ms a frame on WebGPU (up to 6 ms on WebGL 2), with run-to-run swings bigger than the gaps. The pages say so and show draw and triangle counts, which are steady. Only transmission at pixel ratio 1 repeated an order (none 0.9, one side 1.3, double-sided 1.6 ms). Re-timed with **time it** on Oct 14, 2026, two runs each: these four pages cost 0.1 to 1 ms a frame on WebGPU and up to 3.4 on WebGL 2; transmission at pixel ratio 2 keeps its order (0.5–0.7, 0.8–1.0, 1.1–1.2, 1.5–2.4 for none, opacity, one side, double), and at ratio 1 the four are too close to rank. The pages' numbers are the new ones.
- **HDRI floors:** of the repo's panoramas, `blue_photo_studio` (a wood floor) projects well; `studio_small_09` has a plain white floor; the watch's `studio.exr` and `overcast_soil_puresky` have none worth projecting. `camera.far` is raised to 2500 for the sphere.
- **Brushing on the watch:** `anisotropy/brushed.ts` restores WebGi's saved brushing: the cover's map is used as a direction map; the dial's gray map is read as angles (a full turn from black to white) and turned into one. That reading of WebGi's `ROTATION` mode is a guess that looks right (a sunburst around the dial and sub-dials); WebGi's encoding isn't documented.
- **What glass sees, in r186:** WebGPURenderer copies the picture once a frame, just before the first transmissive draw, so see-through parts drawn before it (farther away) are in the copy and later ones, including all other glass, aren't. WebGLRenderer's transmission pass draws only opaque objects (and double-sided glass's back faces). Checked by pixel reads on both of WebGPURenderer's backends; WebGLRenderer from its source.
- **Checked** in the desktop app's Chrome (isolated context, the Vite reload socket stubbed so no reload could log a `track` line), on WebGPU and `&backend=webgl`: every button clicked with no console errors, every scene looked at by eye through canvas captures, and the glass page with two scenes on screen at pixel ratios 1 and 2.

## Bench fixes, and choosing effects for a frame budget

Built Oct 13, 2026: three fixes to the bench, then the last page, in `budget/`.

- **Separate glass copies on every page.** `effectsStage` calls `separateFramebufferCopies()` before any scene draws, so pages no longer call it. Bounced light, both scenes on screen at pixel ratios 2 and 1 (a 1180 × 3200 headless window), every button clicked twice: 750 console errors before ("Texture copy range … touches outside", then invalid command buffers), 0 after on WebGPU, 0 on WebGL 2.
- **The key light's shadow camera is fitted** every frame by `fitShadowCamera` in `stage.ts`: around the watch (and the card when it's up) and where their shadow lands on the floor, plus 0.1. With the bench's light, `near` 5.9, `far` 12.8, 3.6 × 5.1 across, so the bias moves the shadow 0.0035 units (three.js's default box: 0.5 to 500, 10 × 10, 0.25 units; the bench used ±4 until Oct 14, 2026, and now uses r186's real ±5 from `DirectionalLightShadow`). Ambient occlusion's left light: 5.2 to 13.1 (0.004); the ground-shadow page's live light: 6.1 to 11.8 (0.003); the brushed-metal page's sweeping light: 5.2 to 13.7 (0.004); bounced light with the card: 5.7 to 13.7 (0.004). On the contact-shadows page the strap no longer floats once fitted, so contact shadows add little there; with three.js's default box it floats at 2048 and 4096 alike, and contact shadows draw a thin line along the contact. The page gained a shadow camera control (`stage.setShadowFit`) and its text says this.
- **Overlap links:** the baked ground shadow page links the tactile elective's Shadows and grounding, which is now built.
- **The budget bench** (`budget/chain.ts`) builds every effect from a settings object with each page's own settings and helpers, as two chains: one for the moving view, one for the still view.
  - WebGPU allows 32 bytes of color pictures per pixel from one pass, and each 8-bit picture counts 8. With everything on, the scene pass would write five, so roughness rides in the normal picture's spare channel and metalness in the surface color's.
  - On WebGL 2, disposing a pass made r186's `copyTextureToTexture` (two a frame in TRAA) bind the deleted framebuffer it last read from, which it remembers: hundreds of "bindFramebuffer: attempt to use a deleted object" warnings. `chain.ts` disposes old chains three frames late and then clears that memory through the backend's state.
  - `moveWatcher` in `progressive/smoothing.ts` now ignores moves under a millionth of a unit: OrbitControls' damping never quite reaches zero, and at a slow frame rate the view never counted as still.
  - **The stage's GPU timer over-counts frames of many passes on the M4 Pro.** For the WebGi preset it read 150–175 ms a frame on WebGPU (24–26 on WebGL 2) while whole frames took 8–12; the desktop preset 14–15 against 4; the two-pass phone preset 1.7–2.2 against 1.1. The budget page's **time it** draws 30 frames one at a time and waits for each (`queue.onSubmittedWorkDone()` on WebGPU, a fence polled between short yields on WebGL 2). Each timed frame starts a new node frame, `renderer._nodes.nodeFrame.update()`, as the animation loop does: passes run once per node frame, so without it repeated draws skipped their passes and timed almost nothing. Drawing 10 frames per wait then took about 10 times as long as one. The other pages' cost readouts still used the stage's timer then; since Oct 14, 2026 every page times frames this way (see Timing above).
- **Checked** in headless Chrome through Playwright (`channel: 'chrome'`, progress and track writes intercepted, Vite's reload socket stubbed because another session's saves kept reloading the page), on WebGPU and `&backend=webgl`, every page of the elective: every control clicked twice, including the budget page's settings panel, with no console errors. The only warnings were three.js's "Vertex attribute "uv" not found" on the brushed-metal page, which removes the disc's UVs on purpose. Every scene was looked at by eye, and none showed shadow acne at the fitted bias.

## Review fixes

Made Oct 14, 2026, after an independent review of the elective.

- **Cost readouts:** every cost scene times frames with **time it** (see Timing above); every number printed on a page was re-measured that way on both backends at pixel ratios 1 and 2. Reflections high at ratio 2 now reads 14–18 ms (the old readout said 121–152); SSAA ×16 at ratio 1, 6–8 (it said 1.1).
- **Memory on every page visit:** the JS heap grew about 45 MB a page (90 → 603 → 1162 → 1720 MB over three rounds of the 11 pages, after forced garbage collection). Each renderer left a `dispose` listener on r186's shared `DFG_LUT` texture, so every left page stayed reachable. `disposeTslRenderer` in `harness/tsl.ts`, used by every TSL scene and the VFX exercises, takes off the listeners a renderer put on textures and render targets that outlive it, drops the tone-mapping exposure node's hold on it, disposes its post-processing picture's texture, and takes its canvas out of the page, so a renderer that a three.js module variable still holds (an add-on's shared `QuadMesh`, PMREMGenerator's `_oldTarget`) can't hold the left page and its other renderers. `smoothing.ts` gives its shared quad back a blank material after each use. After: 90 → 75 → 76 → 76.5 MB on WebGPU and 92 → 123 → 125 → 124 on WebGL 2; the VFX elective's 25 pages, three rounds, 43 MB flat on both.
- **Shadow map sizes:** a new `mapSize` left render objects bound to the destroyed shadow texture ("Destroyed texture ShadowDepthTexture used in a submit"). `stage.setShadowMapSize` resizes the map between frames and drops every render object; the contact-shadows and ground-shadow pages use it.
- **Key shadow off on the budget page** swaps in a copy of the key light that casts no shadow, instead of setting `castShadow = false`, which disposed the light's shadow while other passes still used it ("Cannot read properties of null (reading 'depthTexture')").
- **Contact shadows off** draws one scene pass with its own depth and motion: no pre-pass, no march.
- **Checked** in headless Chrome through Playwright (`channel: 'chrome'`, progress and track writes intercepted, Vite's reload socket stubbed), on WebGPU and `&backend=webgl` at device pixel ratios 1 and 2: every button of every scene clicked twice, every **time it** pressed, rapid clicks through the budget page's key shadow, presets, and settings, and the reviewer's contact-shadows sequence, with no console errors. Changed scenes were looked at by eye.
